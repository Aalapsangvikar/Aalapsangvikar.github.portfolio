MSc thesis: exploring physics-informed neural networks

University of Limerick, Department of Mathematics and Statistics. Built with PyTorch.

What I investigated

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) learn the solution of a differential equation by building the equation into the training loss. I studied their capabilities and limits on two ODE systems: the damped harmonic oscillator and the SIR epidemic model. For each I tested three settings: forward problems, no-data training, and inverse problems where the model has to recover unknown parameters.

What I built

  • A sine activation on the first layer, which made no-data training possible for the oscillator.
  • A combination of modified initial conditions and warm-start pretraining to solve the SIR no-data case.
  • A data-MSE-floor diagnostic that exposed and corrected a systematic overfitting bias in earlier inverse-PINN implementations.

Breast cancer prediction with XGBoost and explainable AI

Hybrid machine-learning system for mammographic breast cancer detection.

What I built

A system that predicts breast cancer from mammograms and also shows why. I compared several model architectures, namely ViT, CNN, NAS and XGBoost, to capture both local and global image patterns, then chose the best performer.

For transparency, I applied Integrated Gradients and LIME so each prediction highlights the key image regions that drove it. I then wrapped the model in a Swagger UI REST API where a user can upload an image and get a prediction and an explanation back in real time.

Prediction accuracy

XGBoost outperformed ViT, NAS and Hierarchical CNN on the same task.
